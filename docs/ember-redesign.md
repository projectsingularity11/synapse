# Ember redesign

Supersedes the visual and audio implementation described in `cinematic-redesign.md`. The research artifacts, experiment results, and original figure files remain unchanged.

## Experience

Pure-black surfaces, soft-white typography, and red-to-orange ember accents replace the previous palette. Bricolage Grotesque is the variable display face; Space Grotesk handles the interface and reading text. The fixed navigation uses translucent glass on accelerated devices and a less expensive translucent surface on software graphics.

The homepage presents a concrete research question, an original metallic quantum-memory sculpture, a four-step horizontal explanation, and saved-data evidence. The 3D sequence moves from nine data qubits, through schematic check qubits, to actual detector coordinates and a clearly labelled network architecture. Connections in the network are illustrative, not learned weights. The selected history is saved shot 1 at 3% measurement noise; its two events and full-record prediction match the retained record.

Accelerated devices receive instanced Three.js scenes with pointer rotation and detector inspection. Software graphics receive compact opaque WebP renders of the same geometry, with compositor animation and pointer interaction. Rendering pauses offscreen and in hidden tabs. Sustained frame pressure reduces drawing resolution before switching to the poster path. Reduced motion remains user-controlled and persistent.

The persistent water layer uses the MIT-licensed WebGL Fluid Enhanced solver, with red and orange dye, a small simulation grid, and disabled bloom/sunrays. A reusable ring trail and smooth cursor tracker provide the software fallback on every route. Full-screen screen blending is disabled on software graphics because isolated profiling identified it as a compositor bottleneck.

The paper retains its detailed methods, results, confidence analysis, controls, limitations, and references. A three-column desktop layout makes use of horizontal space: active section navigation, the article, and reading tools. Compact reading, progress, citation copying, and printing are available without obscuring the article. The About page uses the owner's supplied education, TSA championships, interests, and contact details; the portfolio remains unlinked.

## Audio and source provenance

Audio starts only after an explicit visitor action. Music is **Airport Lounge**, Kevin MacLeod, Incompetech, ISRC USUAN1100806, CC BY 4.0. The authorized source was reencoded to a 96kbps MP3; no composition was generated. Playback has pause and volume controls, and pauses in hidden tabs. Button effects are original Kenney Interface Sounds recordings under CC0, retained with their license and upstream mirror URLs. Audio attribution is in the sound panel, Credits, and `public/audio/ATTRIBUTION.txt`.

Bricolage Grotesque comes from the official Google Fonts repository under OFL. The original full variable font was compressed to WOFF2 without changing glyphs. Font, music, effect, and rendered-scene hashes are retained in their public provenance manifests. The fluid MIT license and Kenney notice are retained under `public/licenses/`; npm notices are generated by the build. No proprietary reference-site assets or layouts were downloaded into the application.

## Verification

The production build, lint, and five research-data integrity tests passed. All 50 distinct browser checks passed across the full run and focused reruns, including fifteen route accessibility scans, responsive widths, saved-shot replay, links, no-JavaScript reading, optional spatial rendering, audio opt-in/playback/volume, paper tools, reduced motion, touch seeking, and WebGL context loss. The accelerated scene branch is exercised with real software WebGL drawing; physical accelerated hardware is not available in this workspace.

Final screenshots and an isolated six-scenario cadence profile are retained under `docs/qa/ember/`. Reproduce the profile against a production server, without concurrent browser tests or builds:

```sh
TEST_BASE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:3001 node scripts/profile-motion.mjs
```

The profile records 180 requestAnimationFrame intervals during continuous pointer input, held drag, horizontal narrative scrolling, or paper reading. It measures browser scheduling on this workspace's software renderer, not physical display presentation or a minimum guaranteed on every device. The exact results are in `docs/qa/ember/frame-cadence.json`.

Deployment is a review preview of the existing Vercel project. See `deployment.md` for the verified URL and source snapshot. Production aliases and DNS remain unchanged.

The final six-scenario software-renderer sample averages **58.38–60.00Hz**, with **p95 frame intervals of 16.7–16.8ms**, no recorded main-thread long tasks, and 0–3 intervals over 25ms per 180-frame scenario. Horizontal scrolling, mobile story drag, and the paper tracker show no intervals over 25ms in this run. Occasional dropped frames mean this is not a proven hard 60fps minimum.

A screenshot-only paint corruption was isolated to this workspace’s headless GPU compositor: hiding water or changing layers did not reliably remove it, while separate CPU-compositor captures rendered the complete initial page with identical application source. The final timing profile and screenshot captures therefore use Chromium `--disable-gpu` (and retain software WebGL detection). The forced real-WebGL functional tests keep their original graphics setup. This is an execution-environment distinction, not a claimed fix on an untested physical device. The immediately preceding timing run is retained as `frame-cadence-gpu-emulation.json`. Twelve focused checks passed after the last application changes.

The emulated renderer also produced intermittent mobile screenshot paint corruption after changing animation or cursor layers, even with CPU compositing. The raw mobile artifact is retained as a diagnostic; it is not presented as proof of a clean physical-device rendering. DOM/layout/interaction checks pass, but a physical phone review remains necessary. No application CSS was changed merely to hide a screenshot artifact.
