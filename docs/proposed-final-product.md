## The visitor's path

1. **Errors leave traces.** The opening scene shows nine real data-qubit coordinates and the actual saved detection-event pair D7/D15 from simulated shot 1 at 3% measurement noise. A link opens that exact replay. Depth represents time; it is not an unknown quantum state.
2. **Learn what a clue is.** The native readout exercise distinguishes readout bits from changes between them. Its conceptual mode and omitted boundaries are explicit. The real replay uses the circuit's complete detector declarations.
3. **Inspect a real shot.** Choose a measured noise setting and saved shot, reveal history and then outcome, inspect check supports and record offsets, and optionally lift the detector history into 3D. The prediction always uses the complete saved history.
4. **Read the original evidence.** Full-domain responsive error/reliability figures fit a phone without hiding the high-noise endpoint. Counts, Wilson intervals, training seeds and paired results remain available in native disclosures. Calibration-bin occupancy makes sparse evidence visible.
5. **Ask what confidence means.** Separate diagnostics show rare-label prevalence, reference predictors, confusion matrices, and conditional error versus retained coverage. Event probability and confidence in the predicted class have distinct definitions. Diagnostics reuse saved shots; they are not new samples or an operating recommendation.
6. **Challenge the explanation.** A separate equal-budget training control compares fixed-noise and known-test-noise training on fresh shots. Twelve models are frozen before test generation. Gains at high noise and lack of classification-error gain at low noise are both reported. Matched training is an oracle-noise comparator, not automatic adaptation.
7. **Audit and reproduce.** Methods, the actual circuit/DEM, glossary, exact claims, sources, manifests, CSV, vector figures, license notices and scripts expose the complete computational record.

## Scientific findings, with boundaries

The original compact model loses to matching under this simulation protocol. The frozen-model confirmation reproduces the broad pattern on new shots. The original sweep alone combines a harder physical task and training mismatch; it does not isolate their costs.

In the new control at 3% measurement noise, seed 101 has 3,775 fixed-training errors versus 2,736 matched-training errors per 100,000 common fresh shots. Matched-prior matching has 1,838 errors on those same shots. All three initializations improve at higher noise; all three have more classification errors under matched training at the lowest noise. Brier and classification error need not rank models the same way.

Training-data realizations, model capacity, simulator assumptions, oracle information and sample budgets bound every conclusion. These are classical ML experiments for a quantum-error-correction task, not quantum hardware results, AlphaQubit replication or quantum advantage.
